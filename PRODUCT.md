# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary — the traveller in the field.** Two groups, treated as one design subject because
their situation is identical once the trip starts:

1. **The maker and their own travel party.** Waypoint is first personal trip infrastructure;
   the maker researches the guide and then travels on it.
2. **Friends and family the maker builds guides for.** Non-technical readers who receive a
   guide built about *their* trip. They never author, never open a JSON file, and never
   commission the research themselves — the maker does that for them.

**Not designed for (today):** strangers arriving cold. The hub's "＋ Make a new guide" wizard
is the maker's own authoring shortcut that happens to be public — welcome to use, but no
design budget goes to stranger-onboarding until the maker decides to make it a product.
Confirmed 2026-08-04.

**The scene that wins every tiebreak:** on the street, on a phone, mid-trip. One-handed, in
sunlight, possibly offline, possibly lost. When an in-field need and a desk-planning need
conflict, in-field wins.

## Product Purpose

Waypoint produces **verified, personalized travel guides** — never autogenerated from a
model's training data. The four properties a guide must hold (Verified · Personal ·
Actionable · Honest) and the bar test that enforces them are defined ONCE, in `CLAUDE.md`
("What Waypoint Is") — this file does not restate them, so they cannot drift.

Success, as the maker defines it (all four hold simultaneously, confirmed 2026-08-04):

- **Trips go better in the field** — the guide answered the question at the moment it was
  asked; the plan held. The post-trip learnings loop is the scoreboard.
- **Every fact survives scrutiny** — zero unsourced perishable facts; honest blanks where
  research came up short.
- **It demonstrates what is possible** — visibly better than the autogenerated,
  aggregator-fed category (Wanderlog / TripIt class).
- **People outside the maker's circle end up using it** — an aspiration, not yet the design
  target. It does not currently outrank the primary users above.

## Positioning

The mechanism a neighbouring product could not truthfully copy: **the research pipeline is
the backend.** There is no paid travel API and no content generation step. Facts come from
primary sources through a multi-pass research process with automated verification gates, a
fresh-eyes critic pass, and a per-fact source + date stamp that is *rendered to the reader*.
The About page states the position plainly: guides that show their work.

Two consequences that competitors structurally cannot match:

- **Honest blanks are shipped on purpose.** Where research failed, the guide says so rather
  than filling the hole. Surfaces render nothing when there is nothing real to render.
- **Personalization precedes content.** Intake (ranked priorities, dates, party) is captured
  *before* any writing, and the section types are specced from that ranking.

## Operating Context

- **Mid-trip, on a phone.** The dominant read. Offline-capable (PWA precache), one-handed,
  glanceable. Emergencies and "what now" must be reachable in ≤2 taps.
- **Pre-trip, at a desk or shared with the party.** Reading the whole plan, booking,
  budgeting and splitting costs with the group, voting on options.
- **Post-trip.** A feedback survey feeds a learnings loop: curated public post-mortems, the
  maker's private synthesis, and cross-trip traveller patterns that make the *next* guide
  start personalized.
- **Authoring.** The maker edits per-tab JSON files in `src/content/guides/<slug>/`; a
  malformed guide fails the build by design. Every change ends in a fixed ship loop
  (build → lint → typecheck → test → preview → grep `dist/` → push).
- **Sights and Food are repositories, not itinerary echoes.** A traveller who exhausts the
  plan must still have somewhere to go and something to eat, across neighbourhoods, price
  points, and times of day.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed functionality (shipped): per-day itineraries with plan⇄actual toggle · sights and
food repositories with coordinates and a map · transit deep-links per stop · arrival-day
autopilot · verified phrase cards with browser TTS · entry-requirements card · sun/daylight
math · travel-advisory pill (shown only when elevated) · budget pact and live cost split ·
group voting · reminders · booking checklist with T-minus deadlines · SOS sheet · GPX/ICS
exports · OG and post-trip recap image generation · offline PWA.

Technical constraints that bind future work:

- **Static site.** Astro, prebuilt, served by GitHub Pages under a base path — every internal
  `/`-href must go through `import.meta.env.BASE_URL`.
- **$0 API cost.** Paid travel APIs are rejected outright. Free, official sources only.
  Google Places' `place_id` is the one field cacheable indefinitely; nothing else Places
  returns is stored.
- **Third-party SDKs stay behind a config gate + lazy import**, so an unconfigured build is
  inert and the heavy SDK never lands in the main bundle.
- **Enforced attention budget.** Each guide declares a `tabBudget` (default 10) capping
  distinct content groups; the build fails past it. Guides legitimately differ.
- **Perishable facts live in a registry**, not in prose — one row per figure with claim,
  value, source URL, verification date, shelf life, and state; referenced by token and
  substituted at build. An unresolved token fails the build.
- **Shared components are global; only JSON data is per-country.** Any layout or style change
  changes every guide at once, current and future.
- **Tests run zero-network** against real-shaped seed data.

Explicitly undecided: whether Waypoint becomes a product for people outside the maker's
circle, and what would change if it did. Nothing in the current design assumes it will.

## Brand Commitments

- **Name:** Waypoint (repository `Trip-Guides`; deployed under `/Trip-Guides`).
- **Stated promise, already public on the About page:** "Travel guides that show their work."
  Guides are built around real travellers, and every price, hour, and door is checked against
  a primary source and stamped with the date.
- **Voice:** plain, non-technical, addressed to a traveller rather than a reader of software.
  Claims are concrete and checkable; an admitted gap is stated in the open. No marketing
  superlatives about facts the guide has not verified.
- **Numbers on public surfaces are counted at build time, never typed in** — the same honesty
  rule the guides live by, applied to the site's own claims about itself.
- **No CDN.** Fonts and assets are self-hosted and offline-correct.

The visual system exists in code (tokens, base and per-surface stylesheets, self-hosted
variable typography, per-country accent resolution shared across hub, guide, masthead, OG,
and print) **and** in writing: `docs/design-handoff/DESIGN.md` (revision R4, codified from
the shipped Atlas build) is its single authority, with the mechanical gates beside it in
`docs/design-handoff/enforcement/`.

## Evidence on Hand

- **Four real, researched guides** in `src/content/guides/` (denmark, japan, korea, us), each
  with primary-source citations and verification dates. Korea and Denmark additionally carry
  verified phrase cards and entry rows; Korea carries a real post-trip learnings block.
- **Counted proof, generated at build:** guide count, verified-fact count, and distinct
  primary-source count, rendered on the hub and About (`src/lib/guide-stats.ts`).
- **Real post-trip feedback** from at least one completed trip, feeding
  `docs/evidence/traveler-patterns.md` and a guide-level learnings block.
- **Market research** behind the shipped feature set, with sources, in `docs/archive/FEATURES.md`;
  category comparison in `docs/evidence/competitive-landscape.md`.
- **Quality bar and process** are written down: `docs/standards/guide-rubric.md`, `docs/reference/pipeline.md`,
  `docs/reference/motion.md`, `docs/reference/architecture.md`.

**Absences future work must not fabricate:** there are no testimonials, no users, no traffic
or engagement numbers, no revenue, no pricing, no team, and no press. Waypoint has never been
marketed. Any surface implying otherwise would be inventing evidence — which is the exact
failure the product exists to refute.

## Product Principles

1. **Verified or blank — never plausible.** A fact ships with its source and date, or the
   guide says it does not know. This outranks completeness, tidiness, and visual balance.
2. **The traveller on the street outranks the reader at the desk.** When the two conflict,
   design for one hand, bright light, and no signal.
3. **Personal beats comprehensive.** A guide is built around one traveller's ranked
   priorities. Generic coverage is the failure mode, not the safe default.
4. **Surfacing beats sourcing.** If verified data takes more than two taps to reach when it is
   needed, build a surface for it — and never invent data to fill a surface.
5. **Open, not crowded.** The site must feel spacious while carrying everything a traveller
   needs. Merge before adding; the tab budget is enforced, not advisory.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**WCAG 2.1 AA is the binding floor** (enforced in CI via axe-core + Playwright), **plus
in-field conditions** as an explicit additional requirement, confirmed 2026-08-04:

- Sunlight legibility — contrast and type size judged for outdoor phone use, not a dim desk.
- One-handed reach and generous touch targets; gloves and wet hands are realistic.
- Reduced-motion honoured throughout.
- Offline correctness — a guide that fails without signal fails its primary user.
- Non-technical readers: no jargon, no assumed familiarity with the authoring model.
