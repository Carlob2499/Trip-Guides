The Living Atlas

Second pass, four new decisions in. The type sheds its code for faces that belong outdoors. The section titles find a human voice. Navigation splits by device — desktop wears the journey as a horizon; mobile carries it as a spine in the thumb's reach — and one law sits over all of it: the visuals may never outrank the scrolling. Covers stay pathos; the ledger stays ethos.

FOUNDATION · TYPE

First, the type sheds its code

The critique was right: a geometric grotesque and an IDE mono read as software, and Waypoint is not software — it's a field journal. The proposal keeps Literata's book voice and swaps the loud parts: display becomes Fraunces (the warmth of old naturalist covers, with real optical sizes), and data becomes Courier Prime — not code, typed: the expedition log, hammered out at camp.

Honest fallbacks

Video is an enhancement gated on reduced-motion, Save-Data, and in-view. First paint is the poster — today's photo — always.

A1 · Two registers — decided

Covers are pathos: footage from any well-licensed library (Pexels, Coverr, Commons, your own), chosen for feeling, credited top-left, graded toward the guide's accent. The ≈/⚠/Checked apparatus never blesses a cover — it belongs to the ledger below, which stays ethos: every price, hour, and route verified.

A2 · The morph stays cheap

The card→hero View Transition carries the poster; video cross-fades in ~300ms after arrival on the house ease. CLS: video is absolutely positioned, exactly like the photo.

A3 · Control

A visible pause (bottom-right) satisfies WCAG 2.2.2 and taste alike. Reduced-motion users never see motion at all — try the toggle: this mock obeys it.

A4 · Budget

Video never counts against the 200 KB first-paint budget; it's a lazy, hot-linked stream with a ~4 MB ceiling — nothing heavy enters the repo.

B1 · The route is ethos, not pathos

Footage may be liberal; geography may not. The route layer draws only from the guide's map-section pins — it asserts facts. A guide without pins gets contours, never a decorative squiggle.

B2 · The rail keeps its job

Day rail (left) is untouched — story intro, story mode, and the morph already own it. The route-draw is its geographic twin: same accent, same once-per-arrival discipline.

B3 · One fill, two meanings

The accent fill below is simultaneously where you are (station) and how far you've read (the glowing tip creeps within the segment). Today those are two separate widgets.

PLATE 03

The titles find their voice

"Plan · Essentials · Itinerary · References" is CMS furniture — any generator would emit it. The rule that replaces it: a title names what the traveler is doing, not what the database calls the bucket. Two levels: a one-word station for the line and the sheet, a full phrase at the section head — with a typed descriptor so scanning never suffers. Korea, re-titled:

Left — reading is sacred. One section at a time, a compact header that gets out of the way, the next-section band Waypoint already ships, and a trip-aware Today chip floating in thumb reach. No strips, no pill rows, no competing chrome.

Right — the sheet is the spine. The same journey object, turned vertical: every stop with its full title, done-ticks from reading progress, today's leg highlighted inside Day by day. Half-height, grabbable, plain scroll inside — the pattern every maps and booking app already taught your thumb.

Eleven stops don't fit a 375px strip with dignity — so mobile stops pretending to be a strip.

D1 · Four honest destinations

Journey · Today · Map · Kit. Everything else lives inside one of them. SOS keeps its own red post, top-right on every screen — an emergency never hides in a drawer.

D2 · The sheet is the spine

The same journey object as desktop's horizon, turned vertical and moved into thumb range. It's a live table of contents: done-ticks from reading progress, today's leg lit inside Day by day.

D3 · Chrome is still

The law that keeps visuals under UX: motion lives in the covers; chrome never animates on its own. The sheet moves only when a thumb moves it, scrolling is always plain scrolling, and nothing ever animates between the reader and a tap.

D4 · Desktop untouched where it works

Desktop had no crowding problem, so it keeps the one-glance horizon — same tablist semantics, same arrow-key ring. Only mobile changes species.

D1 · Latent structure, not ornament

The leg header (Daejeon → Busan → Seoul · KTX 10:44) is assembled from data every day entry already carries. Nothing is invented — the atlas look is the data made visible.

D2 · The editorial measure

Serif lead at ~62ch does the persuading; the mono rail holds the numbers, flags, and the verification stamp. Readers stop hunting through bold-spans for the price.

D3 · Honesty as a visual system

≈, ⚠, and Checked stamps stop being inline typography and become the rail's grammar — the colophon's signature, extended upward through the whole guide.

THE WAY FORWARD

One programme, five moves

The three-path menu didn't earn its keep, so here's the honest version: a sequence, reorderable, with the foundation first — because every later surface inherits the type and the voice.

R1

Field type + the voice. Swap the faces site-wide (Fraunces / Literata / Courier Prime — self-hosted fontsource, same as today) and re-title the guides to the new standard, with your sign-off per guide. Zero JS, one session, and every screen changes temperature at once.

R2

Mobile goes native. Bottom bar (Journey · Today · Map · Kit), the journey sheet, the floating Today chip; the mobile pill strip retires. The critiqued pain dies first. SOS keeps its post.

R3

Desktop wears the horizon. The line replaces pills on desktop only, absorbs the reading-progress bar, keeps tablist semantics and the arrow-key ring.

R4

Living covers. The pathos layer as already decided — masthead first, then hub. Liberal sourcing, credited, accent-graded, gated on reduced-motion and Save-Data.

R5

The interior atlas. Leg headers from real pins, grid references, the editorial measure with the typed numbers rail.
