### (C) Comparative Workflow Observations
*   **Input Flexibility:** TripAdvisor represents a "closed" workflow where users are tethered to a proprietary database. In contrast, Travel Mapper (via Google Sheets) and Wanderlog allow for more "open" data entry, including custom notes and external links.
*   **Spatial Planning:** A major friction point in TripAdvisor is the inability to see locations *before* adding them to a specific day, preventing spatial optimization during the discovery phase. Roadtrippers and Travel Mapper solve this by keeping the map persistent alongside the list.
*   **Platform Disparity:** There is a recurring trade-off between mobile convenience and planning power. Wanderlog and Travel Mapper are noted for requiring a desktop browser for "serious planning," suggesting that mobile-only workflows currently suffer from information density issues.
*   **Reordering Logic:** TripIt’s lack of drag-and-drop functionality is highlighted as a significant usability regression compared to the modern standards set by Wanderlog and Travel Mapper.

### (D) Counterarguments and Strengths
*   **TripAdvisor:** Despite limitations, it is praised for being "completely free" and having the "most simple user interface."
*   **Travel Mapper:** Offers the highest level of automation (auto-finding addresses) and the best budget-tracking tools for spreadsheet-literate users.
*   **Wanderlog:** Provides the best research integration, pulling context from social media (TikTok/YouTube) directly into the app.
*   **TripIt:** Excels at real-time logistics (flight delays and terminal changes) that the more "manual" planners lack.
*   **Roadtrippers:** The "radius search" for finding stops along a specific route is a unique feature not replicated by general planners.

### (E) Non-Authoritative Cautionary Hypotheses for a Traveler PWA
*   **The "Clutter" Trap:** A PWA attempting to integrate research, maps, and budgeting (like Wanderlog) may become unusable on mobile screens without a highly adaptive "real estate" management system.
*   **API Fragility:** As noted with Wanderlog’s Google Maps export, relying on third-party map hand-offs is a common failure point for reliability.
*   **Offline Failure:** Travel apps frequently fail when users lose data; a PWA must prioritize local caching of the itinerary and map tiles to avoid becoming a "dead" screen in transit.

### (F) Claims That Must Not Become Waypoint Requirements
*   **Mandatory Account Creation for Collaborators:** Forcing every member of a group to sign up (TripAdvisor) is a barrier to entry.
*   **Proprietary Database Restriction:** Users must not be limited to a pre-defined set of POIs; the ability to add custom coordinates/addresses (Airbnbs, etc.) is essential.
*   **Desktop-Only Power Features:** A modern tool should not require a "browser with more real estate" to perform basic itinerary reordering.
*   **Fixed Chronological Sorting:** The inability to easily reorder items (TripIt) should be avoided in favor of flexible drag-and-drop logic.
